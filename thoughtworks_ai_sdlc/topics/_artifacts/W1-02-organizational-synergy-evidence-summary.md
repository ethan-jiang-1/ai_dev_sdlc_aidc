# W1-02 Organizational Synergy Evidence Summary

## Current Thesis

Topic 02 is no longer just “people need to learn AI.” The current evidence package supports a stronger thesis:

- AI mainly shifts the bottleneck from code production to organizational coordination, policy clarity, trust calibration, and change management.
- A new control-plane layer is emerging between coding and executive direction: champions, translators, orchestrators, managers of human-agent workflows, and policy interpreters.
- Developer experience does not disappear in the age of AI, but it is being reweighted toward collaboration, design, learning, and trust.
- Organizations that treat AI adoption as a tooling rollout will underperform organizations that treat it as an operating-model redesign.

## Evidence Clusters

### 1. AI amplifies the organization it enters

- DORA says AI magnifies existing organizational strengths and weaknesses rather than fixing them automatically.
- This means poor coordination, ambiguous policy, and weak platform habits get amplified just like good ones.

### 2. Trust and policy are organizational capabilities

- DORA shows that a clear, communicated AI stance amplifies effectiveness and throughput.
- DORA also shows trust is a major adoption variable, with many developers still trusting AI output only a little.
- Builder mindset research suggests different workers need different levels of control, verification, and abstraction.

### 3. A middle coordination layer is emerging

- GitHub’s AI champions model and executive-support playbook both describe new coordination work: translation, coaching, storytelling, measurement, and feedback loops.
- Thomson Reuters provides a concrete enterprise example where rollout, champions, measurement, and training formed a structured adoption program rather than a mere software rollout.
- Microsoft’s Work Trend Index provides macro evidence that firms are actively considering AI workforce managers and agent specialists.

### 4. DevEx is being reshaped, not erased

- GitHub survey data suggests developers expect AI to increase collaboration, shift value toward design/problem solving, and reduce cognitive effort.
- Microsoft Research shows AI use can also affect onboarding and socialization, not just task completion.
- This supports the seed hypothesis that junior growth paths, staff roles, and identity anchors are all being rewritten.

### 5. The middle-loop tool surface is becoming concrete

- GitHub's workforce playbook shows the human control plane as an explicit operating system of advocates, DRI ownership, communities, policies, and metrics.
- Microsoft's Frontier Firm research shows new AI-focused roles are being actively considered rather than treated as speculative future jobs.
- Atlassian Teamwork Graph shows one plausible shared context layer for middle-loop work: unified cross-tool objects, relationships, permissions, search, automation, and analytics.
- Atlassian Rovo trends and Studio show another concrete operator surface: active users, agent runs, app-level usage, agent libraries, automation rules, and builder workspaces for admins and champions.
- Atlassian's automation model shows that autonomous agent work is being expressed as admin-owned triggers, prompts, downstream actions, and permission-scoped execution boundaries.

## What Is Confirmed

- Human bottlenecks after AI adoption are often about trust, policy, enablement, and coordination.
- Organizations need explicit translator/champion structures to scale adoption well.
- Performance metrics based on code volume become increasingly misleading.
- Human-agent team management is emerging as a real organizational capability area, not just futurist speculation.
- The `middle loop` is starting to look like a real tooling surface composed of graph context, workflow builders, agent analytics, admin-owned automations, and enablement governance.

## What Is Still Missing

- Failure cases showing where middle-loop tooling or enablement programs broke down in practice.
- More detailed evidence on how staff engineers avoid drowning in coordination work.
- More direct evidence on who owns the middle-loop stack day to day once it grows beyond a pilot.
