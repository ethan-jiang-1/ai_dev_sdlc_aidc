## Why This Matters

This source is one of the clearest public descriptions of the human control plane around AI adoption. It is valuable not because it says AI needs champions, but because it spells out an enablement operating model with concrete roles, metrics, policies, and communities.

## Key Facts Captured

- GitHub frames AI adoption as a change-management problem rather than a pure tooling rollout.
- The playbook defines eight mutually reinforcing pillars including executive support, clear policies and guardrails, AI advocates, communities of practice, learning and development, data-driven metrics, right-fit tooling, and a dedicated responsible individual.
- AI advocates are described as internal champions, local experts, community builders, and feedback channels who translate central strategy into team-specific use cases.
- GitHub explicitly formalizes program ownership with a Program Director and Program Manager for the `AI for Everyone` initiative.
- The measurement model goes beyond license assignment and aims to correlate adoption and fluency data with business impact such as productivity, code quality, and developer satisfaction.

## Research Use

- Strong support for the claim that the `middle loop` is a real operating model rather than a loose metaphor.
- Helps define a concrete human control-plane stack: policy, champions, community, DRI ownership, metrics, and training.
- Useful for Topic 02's open question about what organizational surfaces are required to scale AI adoption responsibly.

## Caveats

- This is a playbook from one company and emphasizes enablement over failure cases.
- It is stronger on organizational structure than on day-to-day software tools used by middle-loop operators.
