## Why This Matters

This source is useful because it turns `middle-loop tooling` from an abstract dashboard idea into a concrete platform pattern: a shared object and relationship graph spanning people, work items, documents, messages, and external tools.

## Key Facts Captured

- Atlassian describes Teamwork Graph as a common data model representing work objects across Jira, Confluence, Google Drive, Slack, GitHub, and other systems.
- Connectors map third-party and internal data into shared object types, relationships, and permissions.
- Once data is in Teamwork Graph, it becomes available across Rovo Search, Chat, Agents, and Atlassian Analytics.
- Atlassian explicitly ties the graph to cross-tool automation, unified search, recommendations, and analytics over how work actually flows.

## Research Use

- Strong support for the claim that middle-loop operators need a shared context layer, not just more chat windows.
- Helps define one likely baseline component of the human control plane: a cross-tool graph of work, people, content, and dependencies.
- Connects Topic 02 organizational design with Topic 03 knowledge substrate and runtime context architecture.

## Caveats

- This is a platform capability description, not a field study on adoption outcomes.
- It shows what a tool surface can look like, but not whether teams actually reorganize around it successfully.
