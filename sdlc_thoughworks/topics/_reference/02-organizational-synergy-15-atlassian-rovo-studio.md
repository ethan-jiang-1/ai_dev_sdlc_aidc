## Why This Matters

This source is one of the clearest public signals that the `middle loop` is getting a dedicated tool surface. Rovo Studio is not an end-user chat window; it is a builder workspace for people who shape how work happens across teams.

## Key Facts Captured

- Atlassian positions Rovo Studio as an AI-powered builder workspace for designing, automating, and connecting solutions across work apps.
- The workspace unifies agents, automation rules, and no-code apps in one place.
- Studio uses Teamwork Graph context so builders can create solutions that act with awareness of projects, spaces, coworkers, and customers while respecting permissions.
- Atlassian says Studio is for makers, champions, admins, team leads, and other people who standardize processes, enforce guardrails, and roll out AI-powered helpers.
- Studio explicitly frames itself as the place to turn high-level workflow problems into concrete combinations of agents, automations, and apps.

## Research Use

- Strong support for the claim that middle-loop tooling is likely to look like a builder and control workspace rather than a traditional IDE.
- Helps answer the seed question about what the operator's desk might contain: graph context, agent configuration, automation, governance controls, and reusable workflow assets.
- Useful link between Topic 02 organizational topology and Topic 03 platform architecture.

## Caveats

- This is a vendor product surface, not proof of cross-industry standardization.
- It is stronger on workflow design and governance than on the lived experience of staff engineers working in such a model.
